## Direction contract

The user compares grocery stops around a Japanese train station. White and pale gray surfaces provide readable daylight contrast. Navy type, cobalt action links and orange station notation suggest Japanese rail navigation without a decorative imitation of a map.

Desktop: station chooser and concise explanation above a narrow preferences rail beside store rows. Mobile: station choice and essential filters precede readable single-column store rows. The first store should be findable without a long preference form. No imagery, artificial price charts or fabricated maps.

Store rows separate earning, spending and discounts. Unverified data remains visible as unverified. Dated source disclosure and walking directions stay reachable in each row. Membership selection changes order rather than claiming the cheapest shop. Semantic search is an optional local-model notice finder, with download/loading/error states and no eligibility prediction.

## Quality bar

- Choose a station and find the first store in the first mobile viewport or immediately below it; secondary settings should not occupy multiple screens.
- Read the Japanese branch name, point condition and cash/payment exclusion without opening a dialog.
- Preserve 5–10 researched shops at the original pilot stations. Expanded station coverage must disclose incomplete shop research and provide useful source links; never pad missing coverage with invented branches.
- Use native labelled controls, visible keyboard focus and 44px primary touch targets.
- Pass desktop/mobile overflow checks, date boundaries and real browser model inference.
- No claim of nationwide coverage, independent evaluation or actual outdoor testing.
